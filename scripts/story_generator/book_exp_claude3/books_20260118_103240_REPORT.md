### Story

**The Snap Bird's First Day**

Hot sand burns Pip's claws as she steps to the big mud spot. Her crisp red beak snaps at air—snap, snap, snap. She must find bugs to eat, but the water looks so deep.

A plop echoes. Then another. PLOP.

Something big and gray rises from the mud like a rock. Water drips from its broad back. Pip's wings flutter, but her belly cramps.

The hippo grins. "What do you want, snap bird?"

Pip's voice is small. "I must eat."

"Step on my back," he rumbles. "Scrub the bugs from my spots. I trust you will not chomp me."

Pip steps on. The hippo's skin feels warm. She finds a fat tick and pulls—pop! Sweet juice fills her beak.

The hippo's breathing slows.

Pip scrubs and snaps, her red head bobbing like a bright flower in the crisp sunlight. The reeds whisper their approval.

### Key Lines

- **Worst:** This will not do," she said.
- **Hardest word:** whispered

### Pattern Words Found

Shelly, shell, she, show, shift, sharp, shine, shore, ship, thick, thin, that, thick, chain, check

### Story

```
**The Shell Shop**

Shelly the hermit crab was stuck in her shell. The thick walls pressed against her back and chin. She could not stretch much.

"This will not do," she said.

When the sun came up, she went from rock to rock. The rush of waves made her shell shake. Salt drops hit her chin.

There! A white conch shell sat with the coral. It was big and thick—just what she wished for.

Shelly ran to get it.

But then the sand shifted. A big hermit crab came from back of the coral. He was much bigger than Shelly. His black claws went click, click, click.

"That shell is for me," he said. His voice was deep.

Shelly felt scared. The big crab made a long shadow. She could dash back to her old shell and hide.

Or...

"What if we both look for shells?" she said. "You get the conch. I see a shell by that rock. And the crab there—she wants the shell by the thick weed."

The big crab looked where Shelly pointed.

"The green one by the sharp rocks?"

"Yes! If you help her get that shell, she might give you hers. Then I could have yours, and you get the conch."

The big crab's claws went click, click on the wet rock. A bird sang. Waves whispered on the sand.

"Hm," he said. "A shell chain."

They worked as the sun got higher. The big crab lifted shells from tight spots. Shelly went into small spaces to check if shells were empty. Soon, hermit crabs were switching shells.

When they were done, Shelly stretched wide in her new shell. The walls were smooth and had just the right room. She could see the big crab happy in his conch.

The morning sun hit all the new shells and made them shine like gems on the rocks.
```
